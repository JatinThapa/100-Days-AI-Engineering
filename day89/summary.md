# Day 89 — Reaching the Finish Line of the AI Engineering Journey 🏁🤖

Today was a very special day in my **#100DaysOfAI** journey. After weeks of building, experimenting, revising, and occasionally fighting with datasets and free-model latency 😂, I finally reached the **last day of the program**.

Rather than introducing one completely new concept, today's lesson was about stepping back and looking at **everything I can now build and understand** after the journey.

### 🧠 Looking Back at the AI Engineering Toolkit

Over the course of the program, I worked through a surprisingly broad set of AI engineering concepts.

I revisited how to work with both **frontier and open-source models**, including generating **text, code, images, and audio**, using APIs and Hugging Face-based tooling.

I also built systems using:

🔧 **Tools and Tool Calling**
🧩 **Structured Outputs**
🤖 **AI Assistants and Agents**
📚 **RAG — with and without LangChain**
📊 **RAG and LLM Evaluation**
🧠 **Fine-tuning Open-Source Models**
⚡ **Deploying Models on Serverless AI Infrastructure**
🔗 **Multi-model and Agentic Workflows**

One of the biggest parts of the journey was learning how to approach an AI problem systematically rather than immediately jumping into model selection or coding.

The broader workflow involved things like:

**Understand the problem → Evaluate the data → Build a baseline → Fine-tune when necessary → Evaluate → Deploy**

That process became especially relevant in my own **price estimation project**, where I went from a dataset and baseline models to fine-tuning, RAG, neural networks, model ensembles, and eventually a multi-agent system.

### 🤖 Finally Understanding Agentic AI

The final part of the program focused heavily on **Agentic AI**, and today's lesson tied many of those ideas together.

We revisited three ways of thinking about an agent:

**1. AI systems that can operate autonomously**

**2. Systems where the LLM controls the workflow**

**3. An LLM operating in a loop with tools to achieve a goal**

After everything I built over the last several days, these definitions made much more sense.

My own system now has a loop where the LLM can:

**Decide → Call a tool/agent → Receive the result → Decide again → Continue until the goal is achieved**

I also explored some of the characteristics that make an agentic system more than just a chatbot:

🔹 Breaking a large problem into smaller steps
🔹 Using tools and structured outputs
🔹 Multiple specialized agents collaborating
🔹 A Planning Agent coordinating the workflow
🔹 Autonomous execution
🔹 Memory and state that can persist beyond a single chat interaction

### 🎯 Looking Back at the Journey

What I find most interesting is how these concepts gradually connected.

Early on, I was learning individual building blocks:

**LLMs → Embeddings → RAG → Fine-tuning → Tools → Structured Outputs**

Eventually, those pieces started becoming parts of larger systems:

**Models → Agents → Planning → Tool Calling → Multi-agent Workflows**

And my price estimation project became the place where many of those ideas came together.

What started as individual experiments eventually became a system that could **find deals, estimate their true value using multiple approaches, and send a notification automatically.**

### 💡 Today's Takeaway

Today's biggest lesson wasn't a new API, model, or technique.

It was realizing just how far **understanding the fundamentals and continuously connecting them together** can take you.

I started this journey by learning individual AI concepts. By the end, I was building systems where **multiple models, tools, agents, retrieval systems, and traditional ML components could cooperate toward a single goal.**

There's still a lot more to learn—and this certainly isn't the end of my AI journey—but reaching the end of this program feels like a pretty significant milestone.

After 89 days of learning, coding, debugging, revising, and occasionally asking **“why is this taking 22 more hours?”** 😂, I've finally reached the finish line of the program.

🎯 **Day 89 complete.** 🏁🤖
