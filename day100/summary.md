100 Days of AI — Final Wrap-Up 🚀

After 100 days, my #100DaysOfAI journey officially comes to an end — and looking back, it turned into much more than just learning about AI.

The biggest thing I’m proud of is simple: I didn’t miss a single day. For 100 consecutive days, I showed up, learned something, built something, revised something, or experimented with an idea. Even on days when I was busy, travelling, feeling unwell, or working on something else, I still made sure the journey continued. Some days were deep technical dives, while others were lighter revision days, but there was always something to share.

Throughout these 100 days, I explored a huge part of the modern AI engineering ecosystem. I worked with LLMs, RAG, embeddings, vector databases, chunking and retrieval strategies, fine-tuning, QLoRA, quantization, Hugging Face Transformers, Ollama, OpenRouter, Modal, neural networks, hyperparameter tuning, structured outputs, Pydantic, agents, tool calling, orchestration, APIs and deployment.

But the most valuable part was actually building things around these concepts.

I created a collection of smaller projects and experiments along the way — from LLM-powered chatbots and RAG systems to price prediction experiments, fine-tuned models, serverless AI services, deal-scanning agents, structured-output workflows, and eventually an agentic system that brought several of these ideas together.

One project in particular gradually became a much larger portfolio project. What started as individual experiments eventually evolved into a system combining RAG, proprietary model inference, neural-network predictions, ensemble approaches, agents, tools and deployment. There are still a few practical hurdles left, particularly around API rate limits and payment/billing management, but I'm confident I'll figure those out and get it across the finish line.

Another thing I’m happy about is that the journey wasn't just about collecting buzzwords. I repeatedly went back and revised older concepts. RAG, neural networks, QLoRA, quantization, LLM leaderboards, agent orchestration, Modal deployment and other topics came up multiple times. That repetition helped turn things I had initially learned into concepts I could actually reason about and use.

And perhaps most importantly, these 100 days changed how I look at AI development.

I started with individual concepts and experiments, but gradually began thinking more like a software engineer building AI-powered products — how models connect to applications, how information is retrieved, how agents interact with tools, how systems are deployed, how different components work together, and where the practical limitations actually appear.

So while this challenge ends today, the learning doesn't.

For now, I'm putting #100DaysOfAI on a pause rather than a permanent stop. I want to take a few weeks to relax, look back at everything I've built, clean things up, finish the remaining pieces of my portfolio project, and think carefully about what I want my next learning journey to look like.

The next challenge will be a little different.

I want to combine AI + full-stack development more heavily, while making DSA the primary focus. The plan is to gradually work from easy problems toward medium and harder problems, eventually reaching topics like dynamic programming, while consistently solving around 2–3 problems a day. Alongside that, I'll continue building AI-powered full-stack applications so that the two tracks reinforce each other.

But that's for the next chapter.

For now, I'm just happy that I started this challenge 100 days ago and actually followed it through to the end.

100 days.
100 posts.
Countless experiments.
A lot of debugging.
A lot of learning.
And not a single missed day.

What started as a challenge became a habit.

And that's probably the biggest thing I'm taking away from it. 🚀
